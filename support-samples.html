Customer Support Writing Samples

Author: Emmanuel Adewemimo | Lagos, Nigeria | emmanueladewemimo@gmail.com

These five responses are practice exercises. The company (“Brightly”, an imaginary online software and shop service), the customers and the policies are all made up. Each response is under 150 words, and a short note explains my reasoning.

1. Angry customer: late order

Customer message: “I ordered a week ago and still have nothing. This is the third time you have let me down. I want my money back NOW.”

My response:

Hi Daniel,

I’m sorry your order is late. After waiting a week, your frustration is fair, and I’m sorry this has happened more than once.

I’ve checked your order. It shows as shipped but delayed at the carrier. I’ll ask the carrier for a new delivery date today and email you the update by 5 p.m. If it hasn’t arrived by Friday, I’ll process a full refund, or send a replacement if you prefer.

Would you like me to start the refund now instead of waiting? I can do that straight away.

Thank you for your patience,
Emmanuel

Why I wrote it this way: I start by acknowledging the feeling without arguing. Then I give specific facts, a deadline for my next update, and a clear refund option so the customer keeps control.

2. Technical issue: cannot log in

Customer message: “I can’t log into my account. It says ‘invalid password’ but I just reset it.”

My response:

Hi Amaka,

Thanks for telling me. Let’s get you back in.

Please try these steps in order:
1. Open the reset email you received last and click only that link. Older reset links stop working when a new one is sent.
2. Choose a new password with at least 8 characters, then wait one minute before signing in.
3. If it still fails, try a private (incognito) browser window to rule out saved old passwords.

If none of these work, reply with a screenshot of the error and the email address on the account, and I’ll check it from my side.

Best,
Emmanuel

Why I wrote it this way: Numbered steps are easy to follow, they go from the most likely cause to the least likely, and I say what information I need if the steps fail so the customer doesn’t have to guess.

3. Billing question: charged twice

Customer message: “I see two charges for the same month on my card. Why?”

My response:

Hi Chidi,

Thank you for checking your statement. Seeing two charges is worrying, so let me look into it.

I can see one payment and one “pending authorization” for the same amount. A pending authorization is a temporary hold, not a second charge, and banks normally release it within 3 to 5 business days. If it is still there after that, reply to this email with the date and last four digits of the card, and I’ll send it to our billing team to correct.

You are not paying twice, and I’ll stay on this until it’s resolved.

Kind regards,
Emmanuel

Why I wrote it this way: I explain the likely cause in plain language, give a timeframe, and say what happens if it’s not resolved. I don’t promise a refund before confirming what actually happened.

4. Feature request: dark mode

Customer message: “Please add a dark mode. The app hurts my eyes at night.”

My response:

Hi Sade,

Thank you for the suggestion. Working at night with a bright screen is uncomfortable, and dark mode is a request we hear often.

I’ve logged it with our product team along with your note about eye strain, which helps them see why it matters. I can’t promise a release date, so I won’t give you one. In the meantime, you can lower your phone’s brightness or switch on its night or eye-comfort mode, which also works inside our app.

I’ll let you know if this feature is released.

Best wishes,
Emmanuel

Why I wrote it this way: I take the idea seriously, I’m honest that I can’t promise anything, and I give a temporary workaround so the customer isn’t left with nothing.

5. Escalation: problem still unresolved

Customer message: “This is my fourth email about the same problem. My invoice still shows the wrong company name and my accountant needs it today.”

My response:

Hi Mrs. Okafor,

I’m sorry. You shouldn’t have had to write four times, and I understand the deadline with your accountant.

I’ve read your earlier emails, so you won’t need to explain again. I’m escalating this to our senior billing specialist now with the full history and a note that it’s urgent. You will get a corrected invoice with the right company name from them today. If it hasn’t arrived by 4 p.m., reply here and I’ll chase it myself.

Thank you for your patience,
Emmanuel

Why I wrote it this way: After repeated contacts, the customer needs three things: an apology, proof that I know the history, and a named next step with a time. Escalating with a full summary also saves the next person from asking the customer to repeat everything.
